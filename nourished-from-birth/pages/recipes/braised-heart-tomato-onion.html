Slow-Braised Lamb or Beef Heart with Tomato & Onion

A simple, slow-cooked heart recipe made with onion, tomato, warm spices, and lemon. Lamb heart cooks a little faster, while beef heart usually needs more time to become tender.

This makes a family-size batch. A baby only needs a small portion, so the leftovers are meant for the rest of the family or for the next day.

Lamb heart1½ to 2 hours
Beef heart2 to 3 hours
ServesFamily meal, or several baby portions
Age8 months+

Ingredients

  • 1 lb lamb heart or beef heart, trimmed and cut into small bite-size pieces
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 1 to 2 tbsp butter, ghee, or another cooking fat
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground coriander
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 medium tomato, grated or finely chopped
  • 1½ to 2 cups filtered water or broth, as needed
  • Salt to taste
  • Juice of ½ lemon

Instructions

  1. 1 Trim away any tough connective tissue, large vessels, and excess fat from the heart. Cut into small bite-size pieces.
  2. 2 Heat the butter or ghee in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat.
  3. 3 Add the onion and cook until soft and translucent.
  4. 4 Add the turmeric and stir for about 30 seconds.
  5. 5 Increase the heat to medium-high and add the heart pieces. Cook for several minutes, stirring occasionally, until the outside begins to brown.
  6. 6 Add the coriander and black pepper.
  7. 7 Stir in the tomato paste and cook for about 1 minute.
  8. 8 Add the grated or chopped tomato and cook for another 2 to 3 minutes.
  9. 9 Add enough filtered water or broth to mostly cover the meat.
  10. 10 Bring to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to low and cover.
  11. 11 Cook until very tender: about 1½ to 2 hours for lamb heart, or about 2 to 3 hours for beef heart. Check occasionally and add a little more hot filtered water if the pot becomes too dry.
  12. 12 Add salt during the final 30 minutes of cooking.
  13. 13 Stir in the lemon juice near the end. Taste and adjust the salt or lemon as needed.
Go by tenderness, not the clock: The exact cooking time depends on the size of the pieces and the heart you are using. Beef heart is usually firmer and takes longer than lamb heart, so use tenderness as your final guide.

How I Serve It

For my baby

I mash butternut squash with a generous amount of butter, cut the heart into tiny pieces, and spoon it over the mashed squash. A small portion of heart is plenty for one serving, and the rest of the batch goes to the family.

Other ideas

Serve with soft rice, mashed potato, or tender vegetables, with a little of the tomato and onion cooking liquid spooned over the top.

For babies and toddlers: Cook the heart until very tender. For younger babies, finely chop or shred the meat and moisten it well with the cooking liquid. For toddlers, serve small tender pieces with some of the tomato and onion cooking liquid.

Adaptations by Age

AgeHow to serve
8 to 12 months Finely chop or shred the heart and moisten it well with the cooking liquid. Serve over mashed butternut squash with butter, or with soft rice or mashed potato. Set aside their portion before adding extra salt.
Toddlers Serve small tender pieces with some of the tomato and onion cooking liquid.
Family Serve with rice or mashed potato and extra lemon at the table.

Storage

🧊 Fridge

Up to 2 days in an airtight container. Why we keep cooked meat for 2 days only. Store the meat with some of the cooking liquid to keep it moist, and reheat gently.

Why This Nourishes

❤️
Heart
A lean organ meat that provides protein, iron, zinc, and B12, becoming tender with a long, gentle simmer
🧈
Butter or Ghee
Adds fat to a lean cut, which helps make the dish richer and more satisfying for little ones
🍅
Tomato & Onion
Cook down into a soft, savory sauce that keeps the meat moist and mild
🍋
Lemon
A little juice near the end brightens the flavor of the slow-cooked meat
