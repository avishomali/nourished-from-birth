Persian-Style Slow Simmered Beef Tongue

Prep10 min
Cook3½–4 hrs
Serves6–8
Age6 months+
Storage3 days fridge / 2 months freezer

Ingredients

  • 1 whole beef tongue (2½ to 4 lbs, grass-fed or organic if possible)
  • 1 large yellow onion, quartered
  • 6 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 3 bay leaves
  • 2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 8 to 10 whole black peppercorns
  • 2 tsp sea salt (added during the last 30 minutes)
  • Filtered water, enough to cover by about 2 inches
Optional for serving
  • Ghee, butter, or beef tallow (for searing slices)
Whole peppercorns give a cleaner, clearer broth than ground pepper. Salt added near the end of cooking keeps the meat tender and juicy.
Why beef tongue? Beef tongue is one of the most overlooked cuts of meat in modern kitchens, but it has been a staple of traditional diets across Iran, Mexico, Eastern Europe, and beyond for centuries. It is rich in protein, iron, zinc, vitamin B12, choline, and healthy fats — a genuinely nourishing nose-to-tail food. Slow simmering transforms it into incredibly tender meat while creating a deeply flavorful golden broth. It is one of those dishes where the whole family eats together, each person at their own texture and serving style.

Instructions

  1. 1Rinse the beef tongue well under cold water. Trim away any loose fat or glands at the base if needed.
  2. 2Place the tongue in a large Dutch oven or stockpot. Add onion, garlic, bay leaves, turmeric, and peppercorns.
  3. 3Cover with filtered water by about 2 inches. Bring to a boil, then immediately reduce to the gentlest simmer.
  4. 4Cover and cook for 3½ to 4 hours, or until a knife slides easily into the thickest part with no resistance.
  5. 5Add sea salt during the last 30 minutes of cooking.
  6. 6Transfer the tongue to a cutting board. While it is still warm, peel away the thick outer skin — it comes off easily when warm and becomes much harder to remove once it cools. Trim any excess fat or connective tissue from the base.
  7. 7Slice thinly across the grain. Serve immediately, or lightly sear the slices in butter, ghee, or beef tallow for 1 to 2 minutes per side until lightly golden.

How to Serve

👶 For babies and toddlers

Ladle warm broth into a small cup and let them sip it alongside their meal. Serve thin slices of tongue — the meat is so tender after slow cooking that it requires almost no chewing. For younger babies, blend or finely mash the meat with a little broth.

🥖 For adults

Layer thin slices onto a fresh baguette with sliced tomatoes, pickles, crisp lettuce, and mayonnaise. The combination is classic and one of the best ways to eat tongue.

Do not discard the broth Strain the cooking broth and use it as a base for soups, stews, or to cook rice and grains. It freezes beautifully in portioned containers and is packed with rich golden beef flavor and nutrients. The turmeric gives it a gorgeous color that makes everything it touches taste better.

Storage

🧊 Fridge

Up to 3 days for both tongue and broth, stored separately in sealed containers.

❄️ Freezer

Up to 2 months. Freeze sliced tongue and broth separately in portioned containers.

Tips

  • Peel the tongue while it is still warm — this is the most important practical step. The skin slips off easily when hot and becomes very difficult once the meat cools down.
  • The broth will be deeply golden from the turmeric. This is normal and beautiful.
  • The tongue is done when a knife slides in with no resistance and the meat feels very soft when pressed.
  • Searing the slices in butter or tallow after cooking adds a lightly golden crust and deepens the flavor significantly.
  • Whole peppercorns rather than ground pepper give a cleaner, clearer broth.

Making it Fun

☕ The broth cup — offer warm golden broth in a small cup before the meal. Kids who are hesitant about new foods often warm up to a dish when they start with the familiar comfort of something warm to sip.
🥖 The adult version — while the kids eat their slices with broth, make yourself a tongue sandwich on fresh baguette with tomatoes, pickles, lettuce, and mayo. Same meal, same table, completely different experience.
🌿 Start with the broth — for babies being introduced to beef tongue for the first time, start with a few teaspoons of the warm golden broth. It is flavorful, gentle, and a perfect first introduction to this cut.
