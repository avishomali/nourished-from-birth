Beef & Vegetable Purée

A nourishing, flexible first food combining beef, onion, and soft-cooked vegetables. Adapts easily from a smooth purée at 6 months to a family side dish by 12 months.

Prep10 min
Cook15 min
Serves3 to 4 baby servings
Age6 months+

Ingredients

For the beef
  • 4 oz ground beef
  • 2 tbsp finely diced yellow onion
  • 1 tsp high quality grass-fed butter or ghee
  • A tiny pinch of salt
  • 2 to 4 tbsp warm filtered water or unsalted homemade meat stock or bone broth, as needed
For the vegetables (optional)
  • ¼ cup sweet potato, peeled and diced
  • ¼ cup carrot, peeled and diced
  • ¼ cup broccoli florets
  • ¼ cup peas
  • 1 to 2 tbsp butter or ghee, or more as desired
You can substitute or add other soft-cooked vegetables such as zucchini, pumpkin, butternut squash, cauliflower, potato, or green beans.

Why this recipe is so useful This is one of those recipes that earns its place in a weekly rotation because it is genuinely flexible. Swap the vegetables based on what you have, adjust the texture as your baby grows, and it works just as well as a base for the whole family's dinner as it does a smooth purée for a 6 month old. It is also a good introduction to eating together, since the same beef and vegetables can become everyone's meal with a little extra seasoning set aside for the adults.

Instructions

  1. 1 Cook the beef and onion. Melt 1 teaspoon of butter or ghee in a skillet over medium-low heat. Add the onion and cook until soft and translucent, about 3 to 4 minutes. Add the ground beef and a tiny pinch of salt. Cook thoroughly, breaking the beef into small pieces, until no pink remains and the beef reaches 160°F internally.
  2. 2 Cook the vegetables. While the beef cooks, steam the sweet potato, carrot, broccoli, and peas until very soft. The vegetables should be soft enough to mash easily between your fingers.
  3. 3 Add the butter. Transfer the cooked vegetables to a bowl and add 1 to 2 tablespoons of good quality butter or ghee while they are still warm. Mash together. Add more butter if you want a richer, creamier vegetable mixture.
  4. 4 Combine. Add the cooked beef and vegetables to a food processor or blender. We use a glass jar blender to avoid plastic touching hot food, either the Oster Classic Series or the more budget-friendly Brentwood glass jar blender. Add 2 tablespoons of warm filtered water or unsalted broth. Blend until smooth, adding more liquid a tablespoon at a time until you reach your baby's preferred consistency.
  5. 5 Cool and serve. Allow the purée to cool to a comfortable temperature before serving.

Adaptations by Age

AgeHow to serve
6 to 7 months Blend until very smooth and relatively loose.
7 to 8 months Make it thicker and leave a little more texture as your baby becomes comfortable with thicker foods.
8 to 12 months Mash or pulse instead of completely puréeing. Leave tiny, soft pieces of vegetables and finely crumbled beef.
12 months+ This can become a family side dish or a thicker beef and vegetable mash. Season the adult portion separately as desired.

Vegetable Combinations

You do not have to make the exact same combination every time. That is actually part of what makes this recipe useful.

Sweet & Creamy

Sweet potato, carrot, and peas.

Green

Broccoli, peas, and zucchini.

Autumn

Butternut squash, carrot, and sweet potato.

Simple

Potato, carrot, and peas.

Family Version

Beef, onion, potatoes, carrots, and zucchini.

Storage

🧊 Fridge

Up to 2 days in an airtight container. Why we keep cooked meat for 2 days only.

❄️ Freezer

Freeze in small portions in an ice cube tray or small containers for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before serving.

Busy Mom Shortcut

This recipe does not need to be made exclusively for the baby. When you are cooking ground beef with onion for a family meal, set some aside before heavily seasoning the adult portion. Cook a batch of vegetables at the same time, toss them with butter, and blend everything together for your baby. One pan, two meals.

Why This Nourishes

🥩
Ground Beef
Provides iron, zinc, and B12 in highly bioavailable forms, all critical for the rapid growth happening in the first year
🥕
Root Vegetables
Sweet potato and carrot provide beta-carotene, which converts to vitamin A for immune and vision support
🧈
Butter or Ghee
Adds fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K2, helping a baby's rapidly developing brain and supporting nutrient absorption
🥦
Green Vegetables
Broccoli and peas add fiber, vitamin C, and vitamin K, rounding out the meal with a range of micronutrients
