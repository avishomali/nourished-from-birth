Pear Apple Sauce with Butter and Cinnamon

Four ingredients, twenty minutes, and one of the most naturally nourishing first foods you can make. Sweet, silky, and enriched with grass-fed butter for brain-building fat.

Prep5 min
Cook15 min
Serves6 to 8
Age6 months+
Storage3 days fridge

Ingredients

  • 2 medium organic pears, peeled, cored, and cut into chunks
  • 2 medium organic apples, peeled, cored, and cut into chunks
  • A small splash of filtered water for cooking
To serve — add fresh per portion
  • About ½ tbsp grass-fed butter per serving — we use Kirkland grass-fed butter from Costco
  • A small pinch of Ceylon cinnamon per serving (optional) — we use Frontier Co-op from Whole Foods
Why this works as a first food This is the kind of first food that requires almost no explanation — fruit cooked soft, blended smooth, enriched with good fat. The butter is the key detail that sets this apart from plain fruit puree. A baby's brain is about 60% fat and is growing at an extraordinary rate during the first two years of life. Adding grass-fed butter to fruit purees is a simple way to boost fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K2 alongside the natural sweetness of pear and apple. The cinnamon adds warmth and depth without overpowering the delicate fruit flavor.

Instructions

  1. 1 Prepare the fruit. Peel, core, and cut the pears and apples into even chunks. Keeping the pieces similar in size helps them cook evenly.
  2. 2 Cook until soft. Place the fruit in your glass pot with a small splash of filtered water — just enough to prevent sticking, about 2 to 3 tablespoons. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the fruit is completely soft and breaks apart easily when pressed with a spoon.
  3. 3 Mash or blend. For a very smooth sauce, transfer to a blender or food processor and blend until silky. For a quicker option, mash well with a fork directly in the pot. Let cool then store in the fridge.
  4. 4 At serving time. Spoon 2 to 3 tablespoons of sauce into a bowl. Add about half a tablespoon of grass-fed butter and a small pinch of cinnamon if using. Stir until the butter melts into the warm or room temperature sauce. Serve immediately.
Cinnamon note: Choose Ceylon cinnamon over Cassia cinnamon for regular baby use. Cassia cinnamon, the most common variety found in supermarkets, contains higher levels of coumarin — a compound that can be harmful in large amounts, particularly for young children. Ceylon cinnamon contains significantly less coumarin and is the safer choice for everyday cooking. Frontier Co-op sells Ceylon cinnamon at Whole Foods.
Cooking tip: The longer you cook the fruit, the smoother and silkier the final sauce will be. Cook until the fruit is almost falling apart before blending for the best texture.

Adaptations by Age

AgeHow to serve
6 to 9 months Blend completely smooth. Serve 2 to 3 tablespoons per sitting. Can be mixed into meat stock, stirred into porridge, or served on its own.
9 to 12 months Serve alongside other foods as part of a varied meal. Still a generous pat of butter mixed in at serving time.
12 months+ Serve as a snack, a breakfast alongside yogurt, or a side with any meal. Toddlers enjoy it just as smooth as babies do.
Family Serve as a side dish, stir into oatmeal or porridge, spread on toast, or use as a topping for pancakes. Adults can add a little more cinnamon and a drizzle of honey if desired.

Storage

🧊 Fridge

Store the plain sauce without butter for up to 3 days in an airtight glass container. Add the butter and cinnamon fresh at serving time per portion.

❄️ Freezer

Freeze the plain sauce without butter for up to 3 months. Freeze in small portions in an ice cube tray then transfer to a freezer container. Thaw overnight in the fridge and add butter fresh at serving time.

Tips

  • Buy organic apples and pears. Both appear consistently on the EWG Dirty Dozen list — the produce with the highest pesticide residue. For a recipe made specifically for babies, organic is worth it here.
  • Use ripe fruit for the best natural sweetness. Overripe pears and apples that are too soft to eat fresh are perfect for this recipe.
  • A glass pot is ideal for this recipe — no coating concerns and easy to see when the fruit is ready.
  • Use filtered water for cooking. Even a small amount of tap water goes into the sauce and chlorine is worth avoiding for baby food.
  • Ceylon cinnamon has a milder, more delicate flavor than Cassia. Start with a very small pinch for young babies and adjust to taste as they get older.
  • The butter amount can be adjusted up as your baby gets older and used to the richness. A full tablespoon of butter for 2 to 3 tablespoons of sauce is completely appropriate for a baby whose brain is growing rapidly.
  • Mix varieties of apple and pear for more complex flavor. Bosc pears and Fuji apples work particularly well together.
  • You can also make this with just apples or just pears — both work beautifully on their own. A pure pear sauce is particularly gentle for very young babies just starting solids.

Why This Nourishes

🧈
Brain-Building Fat
Grass-fed butter provides fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K2 alongside butyrate — all critical for brain development and immune function in the first two years
🍐
Natural Sweetness
Pears and apples provide natural fructose, fiber, and vitamin C without any added sugar — training the palate toward whole food sweetness from the very beginning
🌿
Ceylon Cinnamon
Gently anti-inflammatory, traditionally used to support digestion, and lower in coumarin than Cassia — a safe and nourishing spice introduction for young babies
🫧
Gentle Fiber
Cooked pear and apple provide soluble fiber that supports healthy digestion and feeds beneficial gut bacteria — gentle enough for the youngest eaters
