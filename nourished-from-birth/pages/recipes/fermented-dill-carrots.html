Fermented Dill Carrots

Three ingredients, one jar, and about a week of patience. One of the best introductory fermented foods for toddlers — naturally sweet, tangy, and deeply nourishing for the gut microbiome.

Prep10 min
Ferment5 to 14 days
Jar size32 oz mason jar
KeepsUp to 6 months

Ingredients

  • 1 lb carrots, peeled and cut into sticks of similar size
  • 1 tbsp mineral sea salt — non-iodized only
  • 3 cloves garlic, peeled and left whole
  • 1 tsp dried dill or 2 to 3 sprigs fresh dill
  • Filtered water to cover — enough to fill the jar leaving 1 inch of headspace

Why fermented carrots? Carrots are one of the best vegetables to start with for home fermentation. Their natural sweetness mellows and deepens during fermentation, making them appealing to young children who might reject more pungent ferments like sauerkraut or kimchi. The texture softens slightly but stays satisfyingly crunchy. And the process itself is about as simple as fermentation gets — pack a jar, add brine, wait. The result is a living food rich in lactobacillus bacteria that seed and support the gut microbiome during the critical first years of life.

What you will need

  • 32 oz wide-mouth mason jar with lid — Ball jars are what we use and trust for fermentation
  • Glass fermentation weights — these keep the carrots fully submerged under the brine. A small ziplock bag filled with brine or a clean cabbage leaf also work in a pinch
  • Airlock lid (optional but helpful) — allows gases to escape without letting air in. A regular lid works too, just open it briefly once a day to release pressure

Instructions

  1. 1 Sterilize the jar. Wash the mason jar and lid with hot soapy water and rinse thoroughly. No need to boil — a good hot wash is sufficient.
  2. 2 Prepare the carrots. Peel the carrots and cut them into sticks of similar size — this ensures they ferment evenly. Make sure they are short enough to fit in the jar with at least an inch of headspace above.
  3. 3 Layer the jar. Add the garlic cloves and dill to the bottom of the jar first. Then pack the carrot sticks in vertically and tightly — you want them wedged in so they do not float up later. Pack them as full as you can.
  4. 4 Add the brine. Dissolve the sea salt in enough filtered water to fill the jar, then pour it over the carrots. The carrots must be fully submerged. Leave about 1 inch of headspace at the top. Place the fermentation weight on top to keep the carrots under the brine.
  5. 5 Close and ferment. Seal the jar with an airlock lid or a regular lid. Store at room temperature away from direct sunlight. Ferment for 3 to 14 days — we find around 7 days is the sweet spot. The longer you ferment, the tangier the carrots will become. Taste from day 3 onwards until you reach your preferred flavor.
  6. 6 Refrigerate. Once they reach the flavor you like, remove the fermentation weight, seal tightly, and move to the fridge. They will continue to ferment very slowly in the cold but stay at a stable level of tanginess.
What to expect: Cloudy brine is a good sign — it means active lacto-fermentation is happening. Clear brine means fermentation has not started yet. White sediment at the bottom of the jar is completely normal and is just settled bacteria. If you see pink or black mold or smell anything unpleasant, discard and start again.
Salt note: Use only non-iodized mineral salt or sea salt. Iodine inhibits the lactobacillus bacteria that drive fermentation. Table salt with iodine will prevent the fermentation from working properly. Also use filtered water — chlorine in tap water kills the beneficial bacteria you are trying to cultivate.

Adaptations by Age

AgeHow to serve
6 months+ Offer the brine only — start with just half a teaspoon mixed into food or offered on a spoon. The brine contains live cultures without the choking hazard of the carrot stick itself. This is a gentle and effective way to introduce probiotics from fermented vegetables very early.
12 to 16 months Continue with the brine. Hold off on the carrot sticks until chewing skills are well established. Even softened from fermentation, a carrot stick still requires solid chewing ability.
16 months+ Offer the carrot sticks once your toddler has reliable chewing skills. Cut into smaller pieces if needed. Serve alongside meals as a tangy, crunchy side.
Family Serve straight from the jar as a snack, alongside grain bowls, next to Persian rice dishes, or as part of a mezze spread.

Storage

🧊 Fridge

Up to 6 months in the refrigerator once fermentation is complete and the jar is sealed. The carrots will continue to ferment very slowly in the fridge — the flavor will deepen over time. Do not freeze — the texture will change significantly and the live cultures will be destroyed.

Tips

  • Seven days at room temperature is the sweet spot for flavor. Taste from day 3 and refrigerate when the tanginess is where you want it.
  • Use only non-iodized salt and filtered water. These two things are the most common reasons fermentation fails.
  • Keep the carrots fully submerged under the brine at all times. Exposure to air is where mold develops. The fermentation weight is not optional — use something to keep the carrots down.
  • If using a regular lid instead of an airlock, open it briefly once a day to release the gas that builds up during fermentation. This is called burping the jar. With an airlock lid this is not necessary.
  • The brine is just as valuable as the carrots. Do not discard it — offer it to babies from 6 months, use it as a salad dressing, or add it to other dishes for a probiotic boost.
  • Fresh dill gives a brighter flavor than dried. If you can find it, use it. Either works.
  • Make sure carrot sticks are cut to a similar size so they ferment evenly.

Why This Nourishes

🦠
Live Probiotics
Lacto-fermentation produces lactobacillus bacteria that seed the gut microbiome and support immune function, particularly important during the first 1,000 days
🥕
Beta-Carotene
Carrots are rich in beta-carotene which converts to vitamin A — essential for immune function, vision development, and healthy skin in babies and toddlers
💊
Enhanced Nutrient Absorption
Fermentation breaks down anti-nutrients and produces enzymes that make the carrots easier to digest and their nutrients more bioavailable
🍽️
Microbiome Diversity
Introducing a variety of fermented foods early establishes a more diverse gut microbiome — linked to better immunity, metabolic health, and even mood regulation
