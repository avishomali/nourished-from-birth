Cod Liver Oil for Kids: Why It Matters and How to Give It

What cod liver oil actually contains, why those nutrients matter for babies and toddlers, how to give it safely, and the one brand worth recommending.

7 min read Nutrition · Supplements · First Year

Cod liver oil is one of those supplements with a long history and a lot of noise around it. Traditional cultures across Scandinavia gave it to children for generations, not because they understood vitamins, but because they observed that it worked. Today we know what is in it. What is less clear is how to cut through the marketing, the misinformation, and the very real safety considerations to make a thoughtful decision for your own child.

Both of my kids have taken Rosita cod liver oil since they were 6 months old. I give it to them with a small oral syringe, straight, no mixing. I started early enough that they never knew it was supposed to taste bad, and now it is just part of their daily routine.

This article covers what cod liver oil actually contains, why those nutrients matter, how to give it safely, and the one brand worth recommending.

What's in cod liver oil

Cod liver oil delivers three nutrients that are genuinely difficult to get in adequate amounts from the modern diet:

Vitamin D

Most children in northern climates do not get enough sun exposure to maintain adequate vitamin D levels, especially in winter. Vitamin D supports calcium absorption for bone development and plays an important role in immune regulation. It is one of the most common nutritional deficiencies in children.

Vitamin A (preformed)

This is the form of vitamin A the body actually uses directly. It is not beta-carotene from vegetables, which must be converted and which many people (especially young children) convert inefficiently. Preformed vitamin A supports immune function, eye health, and the integrity of mucus membranes. It occurs naturally in liver, egg yolks, and fatty fish, foods that are increasingly absent from children's diets.

Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA)

DHA is a primary structural component of the brain and retina, and children need a steady supply during the first years of life when brain development is most rapid. The European Food Safety Authority recommends 100mg of DHA daily for infants 7 to 24 months, and 250mg of EPA and DHA combined for children over two. Most children do not reach these amounts through diet alone.

Why natural matters

Most cod liver oils on the market are heavily processed. The natural vitamins are stripped out during refining and synthetic versions are added back in. This matters because synthetic vitamin A behaves differently in the body than naturally occurring vitamin A, and at high doses carries greater toxicity risk. A high-quality, minimally processed cod liver oil delivers these nutrients in their natural ratios and forms, the way they have always been consumed.

Safety considerations

Cod liver oil is safe at appropriate doses, but vitamin A and vitamin D are fat-soluble. They accumulate in the body over time and can reach toxic levels if given in excess. This is not a theoretical risk. In 2025, children in the US were hospitalized with vitamin A toxicity linked to excessive cod liver oil use. Dosage matters significantly.

⚠️ Important safety points

  • Always use a proper measuring device. A kitchen spoon is not accurate enough.
  • Do not combine with other supplements containing vitamins A or D without guidance from your pediatrician.
  • Cod liver oil is not a treatment or preventive for any disease.
  • Always consult your pediatrician before starting, especially for infants under 6 months.
  • Do not exceed recommended doses.

Dosage guidelines

The following are general reference doses. Always confirm with your pediatrician for your specific child.

6 to 12 months½ tsp daily

4 years and up1 to 2 tsp daily

How to give it to kids

I use a small oral syringe for both of my kids. It gives precise dosing and makes it quick. I started at 6 months before they had any preconceptions about the taste, and it has never been a battle. A syringe is especially practical for babies who cannot drink from a spoon yet.

If you are starting with an older child who is resistant, a few approaches that work:

  • Mix into a small amount of orange juice. Citrus cuts the fishy taste significantly.
  • Stir into yogurt or unsweetened applesauce.
  • Add to a smoothie.
  • Some children will take it straight from a spoon, especially if started early.
  • Softgel capsules are an option for older children who can swallow them.

The bottom line

Cod liver oil fills genuine nutritional gaps that are hard to close through diet alone in modern life, particularly vitamin D in northern climates and DHA for brain development. At appropriate doses from a quality source, it is a practical addition to a child's daily routine.

Start small, dose carefully, use a quality source, and work with your pediatrician. Do not use it as a substitute for medical care, and do not exceed recommended doses.
